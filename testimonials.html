Child Healthcare & Nutrition

Elvis

Elvis is two years old, but he can barely walk. His mother was only 13 when he was born, and she left him in the care of her grandmother, who is not able to look after him.

Elvis is severely malnourished. The family has almost nothing to eat. Most weeks they eat only about three times, on the days someone shows them mercy and brings them food.

Children like Elvis are why our Child Healthcare & Nutrition programme exists: to make sure no child grows up hungry and forgotten.

Teen Mothers

Namambo Shakirah

Namambo Shakirah is a 16-year-old teenage mother from Kilisiru Village, Kayunga. At an age when many young people are still finding their way through school and adolescence, Shakirah is already carrying the responsibility of caring for a child.

Her journey has become especially difficult because the partner who should have shared the responsibility of raising their child abandoned her. She is now facing motherhood without that support. Being so young and having to carry these responsibilities can leave a teenage mother feeling isolated, uncertain and overwhelmed.

Shakirah’s story is a reminder that teenage mothers need more than sympathy: they need practical help, encouragement and people who are willing to stand beside them. With support, she can get essential items for herself and her baby, receive guidance and encouragement, and have a better chance of building a stable future.

Any contribution, whether financial, essential supplies, baby items or other help, can make a meaningful difference. No act of kindness is too small.

No teenage mother should have to face motherhood alone.

Orphans

Namubiru Esther

Namubiru Esther is an orphan. She was still in Primary Six when she became pregnant at the age of 16. When her pregnancy became known, her grandmother chased her away from home, leaving her without the family support and security she needed at such a difficult time.

The father of the baby, who is also underage, denied the pregnancy and has not taken responsibility. Esther is now staying with a friend. Without a stable home or reliable support, she faces an uncertain future as she prepares to care for her baby.

Esther needs safe accommodation, essential supplies for mother and baby, emotional support, guidance, and the chance to continue her education when circumstances allow. We believe a young mother’s circumstances should not define or destroy her future. Esther deserves compassion, protection, dignity and practical help, not rejection or judgement.

Contributions towards basic needs, baby supplies, safe accommodation, counselling and support to return to school can make a real difference. Every gift, however small, helps her take a step towards a safer and more hopeful future.

Together, we can help Esther find safety, support and a second chance.

Pregnant Women

Malita Isha

Malita Isha is 25 years old and four months pregnant. She lives with her husband, and although they are doing their best to earn a living, both are facing hardship.

Their home is in a worrying condition and is reportedly close to collapsing. That leaves the couple unsure how safe the place they live really is, at a time when Malita needs care, security and support during her pregnancy.

The couple are struggling to meet their everyday needs. Malita also needs help to access antenatal care, prescribed medicines and other pregnancy essentials, as well as the basic items she and her baby will need in the months ahead.

Support could help the family reach antenatal services and any medicines prescribed by a qualified health professional, buy nutritious food and essential supplies for mother and baby, and deal with the urgent safety concerns about their home. Every contribution, whether financial, material or practical, helps.

Together, we can help make this pregnancy safer and this family’s home more secure.

From the field · Education

Restoring hope and smiles at Kilisiru Primary School

Kilisiru village, Kayunga District, Uganda

Joy and gratitude filled the grounds of Kilisiru Primary School after an outreach visit by Dee the Samaritan Charity Foundation.

Driven by our mission to restore peace, dignity and God’s love to vulnerable communities, we visited the school to give young learners essential scholastic materials and treats. For many children in rural communities, having basic learning tools like exercise books makes the difference between struggling in class and pursuing their education with confidence.

During the visit, dozens of excited pupils gathered on the school compound, proudly raising their new books and snacks into the air. In a touching display of appreciation, they held up a handmade sign thanking the Foundation for standing with them on their educational journey.

Through outreach like this, we continue to bridge gaps in rural education and bring real hope to families across Uganda.
